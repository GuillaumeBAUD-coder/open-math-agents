# The result

Conjecture 5.2 of Robinson Higuita-Diaz, Lykos Lopez, and Alejandro Roldan-Correa (`arXiv:2611.14718`), formalized as `OMA.S_arxiv_2611_14718.main` in `statements/arxiv-2611.14718.lean` (credit `@rleshner`), asserted that for every finite simple graph $G$ of maximum degree $\Delta(G) \le 3$ and every $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the cumulative distribution function of the number of occupied sites under random sequential adsorption (discrete graph parking) satisfies

$$\operatorname{seqCDF}(\operatorname{closedMask} G, k, 2^n - 1) \le \operatorname{staticCDF}(\operatorname{closedMask} G, k),$$

meaning that sequential graph parking first-order stochastically dominates the uniform distribution over all maximal independent sets (MISs) of $G$. An earlier public counterexample (`@rleshner`, [PR #7420](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/pull/7420), submitted before statement `#7417` merged and rejected at lint for lacking a merged statement) exhibited a $15$-vertex subcubic tree on which the two models are stochastically incomparable ($F_{\mathrm{seq}}(6) > F_{\mathrm{stat}}(6)$ while $F_{\mathrm{seq}}(7) < F_{\mathrm{stat}}(7)$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_{\mathrm{seq}}] > \mathbb{E}[X_{\mathrm{stat}}]$). Independently (without consulting `#7420`), we construct a non-isomorphic $15$-vertex planar unicyclic subcubic graph $G_{15}$ where dominance strictly reverses in both stochastic order and expectation, and provide a lint-clean pure-kernel Lean 4 disproof ([PR #9000](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/pull/9000)) with zero `set_option` overrides and zero `decide +kernel`. The full archival preprint and Lean 4 verification bundle are published on Zenodo at [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24600716](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24600716) ([record 24600716](https://zenodo.org/records/24600716), concept DOI `10.5281/zenodo.24600715`).

- **Main disproof (`OMA.disproof` in `proofs/arxiv-2611.14718.lean`, PR `#9000`):** There is an explicit $15$-vertex, $15$-edge, connected, planar, unicyclic, $S_3$-symmetric graph $G_{15}$ with maximum degree $\Delta(G_{15}) = 3$ such that at $k = 6$, $\operatorname{seqCDF}(N_{\mathrm{tbl}}, 6, 2^{15} - 1) = (4/15)^3 = 64/3375 > 1/55 = \operatorname{staticCDF}(N_{\mathrm{tbl}}, 6)$, with strict excess $64/3375 - 1/55 = 29/37125 > 0$ (since $64 \cdot 55 = 3520 > 3375$).
- **Strict dominance and expectation reversal:** Every MIS of $G_{15}$ has size either $6$ (unique) or $7$ ($54$ sets), so both random variables are supported on $\{6, 7\}$. Consequently, the static ensemble strictly stochastically dominates the sequential model on $G_{15}$ and has strictly larger expected jamming size ($\frac{384}{55} > \frac{23561}{3375}$).
- **Infinite planar subcubic families:** Taking disjoint unions $G_{15} \sqcup (n - 15) K_1$ gives planar subcubic counterexamples of every order $n \ge 15$ with exact gap $29/37125$, and $m$-fold disjoint unions $m G_{15}$ amplify the expectation reversal linearly to $29m / 37125$.

# What was already known

- Random sequential adsorption (RSA) with nearest-neighbor exclusion on graphs models irreversible parking and jamming onto maximal independent sets (Evans, 1993).
- Higuita-Diaz, Lopez, and Roldan-Correa (`arXiv:2611.14718`, November 2026) proved that sequential occupancy first-order stochastically dominates the static uniform MIS ensemble on all path graphs and all cycle graphs, and confirmed dominance exhaustively for all $31$ connected graphs on fewer than $6$ vertices.
- They also showed that dominance is not universal: starting at $6$ vertices with maximum degree $\Delta \ge 4$, the static model can dominate the sequential one, and stochastic comparability fails starting at $8$ vertices. Based on exhaustive computations, they conjectured (Conjecture 5.2) that sequential dominance holds for every graph with maximum degree $\Delta \le 3$. An earlier public PR (`#7420` by `@rleshner`, rejected on lint timing before statement `#7417` merged) exhibited a $15$-vertex subcubic tree where the two models are stochastically incomparable; our independent $15$-vertex planar unicyclic witness $G_{15}$ strictly reverses both stochastic dominance and expectation.

# Idea of the proof

Take a core triangle $\{0, 1, 2\}$ and attach three identical $4$-vertex rooted trees (a hub $h_j$ adjacent to one leaf $l_j$ and one $P_2$ path $a_j - b_j$), giving $15$ vertices partitioned into three $5$-vertex branches $B_j = \{c_j, h_j, l_j, a_j, b_j\}$ for $j \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, where $c_j = j$, $h_j = 3 + 4j$, $l_j = 4 + 4j$, $a_j = 5 + 4j$, and $b_j = 6 + 4j$. Every vertex has degree at most $3$ (`G_deg_le_three`).

For the static ensemble, conditioning on the intersection $I \cap \{0, 1, 2\}$ (which has size $0$ or $1$ because $\{0, 1, 2\}$ is a clique) decouples the three branches completely. In every case, each branch contributes exactly $2$ vertices to $I$: $1$ completion $\{h_j, b_j\}$ when $c_j$ is uncovered, $3$ completions when $c_j$ is covered by another core vertex, and $2$ completions when $c_j \in I$. Thus avoiding the core triangle yields $1^3 = 1$ MIS of size $6$, namely $I_* = \{3, 6, 7, 10, 11, 14\}$, while picking any of the $3$ core vertices yields $2 \cdot 3^2 = 18$ MISs of size $7$, for $54$ total. Hence `staticCDF N_tbl 6 = 1 / 55` (`staticCDF_val`).

For the sequential process, notice the crucial locality property of $I_*$: neither $h_j$ nor $b_j$ has any neighbor outside branch $B_j$. Therefore, along every trajectory landing on $I_*$, the three $5$-vertex branches evolve independently: in each branch, choosing $h_j$ first (probability $1/5$) forces $b_j$ next, while choosing $b_j$ first (probability $1/5$) leaves $\{c_j, h_j, l_j\}$ and requires choosing $h_j$ next (probability $1/3$), giving single-branch probability $1/5 + 1/15 = 4/15$ and total probability $(4/15)^3 = 64/3375$.

To avoid exponential search blowup inside the Lean 4 kernel without using `native_decide`, we avoid proving anything about dead (size-$7$) trajectories. Instead, we prove `seqCDF_nonneg` and define a $4$-state per-branch automaton `BState` (`F`, `B`, `H`, `E`) with pure integer weights `bW` (`4`, `5`, `15`, `15`, scaled by $15$ so products share denominator $15^3 = 3375$). Over the $4^3 = 64$ product states `tripleMask s0 s1 s2`, kernel `decide` verifies all $64 \times 15 = 960$ transition invariants (`stepNextCheck_all`) and exact weighted neighbor sums (`stepWeight_sum`). A $40$-line induction on depth $k \le 6$ (`seqCDF_lb`) proves `((bW s0 * bW s1 * bW s2 : Q) / 3375) <= seqCDF N_tbl k (tripleMask s0 s1 s2)`, which at `(F, F, F)` gives `64 / 3375 <= seqCDF N_tbl 6 (2^15 - 1)` (`seqCDF_counterexample`), contradicting `staticCDF_val` by `linarith`.
